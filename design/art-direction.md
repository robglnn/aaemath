# Art direction — Variable Star

Binding for every builder who writes a shader, a material, a light, a post pass, a UI surface, a
mesh or a camera framing.

> **The render target is `reference/target-lowpoly.png`. Open it before you read another line.**
> Flat-shaded low-poly: PS1-era geometry lit with modern lighting. Faceted solids, per-face flat
> shading, hard silhouette edges, a warm dusk sky with hard-edged stylised cloud slabs, warm ochre
> rock in light against a chromatic blue-grey in shadow, cyan crystal and cyan water as the only
> saturated accents, hard-edged blocky UI, and bare white KaTeX floating unframed in world space.
>
> `reference/brief-hero.png` is retained as a **mood** artefact only — colour feeling, sense of
> wonder. It is not the render target. **Where the two conflict, the low-poly target wins**, and
> every painterly rule this document used to carry — PBR material richness, normal and roughness
> maps, smooth organic surfaces, glass hologram panels, screen-space ink, a filmic shoulder, violet
> shadows — is now *wrong* and has been deleted rather than softened.
>
> This target is achievable in real time. "The reference is a painting" is no longer available as an
> excuse. We are expected to match it and then beat it, and beating it looks like: better
> composition, better light, and motion and life a still cannot have.

`design/palette.json` is the machine-readable half of this document. **It is machine-written**: no
colour in it was typed by hand. Regenerate it, and every number in this file, with

```bash
node review/p02-r5-sample.mjs                     # print the whole measurement table
node review/p02-r5-sample.mjs --write-palette     # rewrite design/palette.json from the target
node review/p02-r5-sample.mjs --check <shot.png>  # run §13's arithmetic against one of OUR captures
```

`--check` is the point of the whole document: it runs the same predicates, through the same decoder,
against a real capture, and prints a pass/fail scoreboard.

**The first thing `--check` must do is pass on the binding reference.** A gate the target itself
fails is not a gate, it is a bug with a scoreboard attached, and chasing it moves the build *away*
from the target. So:

```
node review/p02-r5-sample.mjs --check reference/target-lowpoly.png   →  12 / 12
node review/p02-r5-sample.mjs --check review/shots/p02/r2-check.png  →   2 / 12
```

The second line is a real 1920×1080 capture of the current build (`play:1;look:200:-60`, report
`problems: []`). **The build scores 2 / 12 and the target scores 12 / 12; that distance is the
work.** Re-run both after any change to §13, in that order. If the target ever drops below 12 / 12,
the row that dropped it is wrong and this document must be fixed before anybody touches a shader.

Every constant below is followed by the box or predicate that produced it, or is marked **derived**
(arithmetic on two measurements, with the arithmetic printed) or **authored** (a judgement, with the
reasoning printed). Prior rounds of this project were demolished for publishing constants a critic
could not reproduce. If a number here cannot be regenerated by that command, it is a bug — say so in
your handoff rather than working around it.

## 0. Three classes of number, and one line on which is which

| class | what it is | how many are here | how to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sampled** | read straight off `target-lowpoly.png` by a stated median-of-box or median-of-mask | **42 roles** + the whole census + 21 laws | re-run the sampler |
| **derived** | a ratio or division of two sampled values; the formula is printed beside the answer | the light rig, the value ladder, the two plate alphas, the three constructed roles | redo the division |
| **authored** | a judgement the target cannot answer, because it is one still frame of one camera with nothing moving in it | every budget *range*, the geometry counts in §2, all of §11 | argue with it |

Nothing in this document is a vibe. Where it is a judgement, it says so and gives its reason.

**A published number carries its WHOLE predicate, not most of it.** A y-window without its x-window,
a luminance gate without its hue gate, a mask without its rounding convention — each of those is a
number a critic cannot regenerate, and each of them has already cost this document a round. Three
specific conventions, all of them load-bearing, all of them now printed in
`palette.json → howToRegenerate.samplingRules`:

- **Bands publish x as well as y.** `laws.depthBands` is x ∈ [0.25, 0.85] (§7.3). Full width answers
  a different question and returns different numbers.
- **Hue is never rounded inside a predicate**, only for display. Rounding first moves
  `world.foliage.lit` from `#585939` to `#595A3A`.
- **A dark-pixel gate names its hue window**, because the dark end of this picture is *bimodal* by
  law (§3.4, §13 rows 3–4).

## 1. What the target actually is

A low sun just off the right edge of frame at the horizon. A young courier in faceted work armour
stands with her back to us on an ochre shelf. A hard-edged rock spire cuts the left third. A cyan
river winds through a warm plain toward a floating shelf carrying a city. Cyan crystal clusters glow
in the near and middle ground. Above it all a warm dusk sky, teal at the top, with stylised cloud
slabs cut out of it in hard steps. `x + 3 = 7` hangs in the air in bare white, next to a white
stair-stepped graph, with no panel behind either.

**Six facts do all the work, and all six are measured.**

1. **A facet is exactly one colour.** Scan 110 px across one lit plane at `y=0.24, x 0.100→0.140` and
   the luminance moves from **0.2678 to 0.2774** — a spread of **0.0096**, which is dither, not
   shading. Scan 84 px down another plane and the spread is **0.0109**. *There is no gradient across
   a face anywhere in this image.*
2. **The value ladder is the cosine ladder.** The lit facets of the foreground rock mass sit at
   Y **0.4435 / 0.3129 / 0.2720 / 0.2398 / 0.1192**, and a face turned from the key sits at
   **0.0227**. Divide by the brightest: **1.00 / 0.71 / 0.61 / 0.54 / 0.27 / 0.05**. That is N·L,
   undistorted. **There is no tone curve in this picture** (§3.5).
3. **A cliff resolves into countable planes.** Flood-filling the foreground rock mass at a tolerance
   of 2/255 finds **43 compact planes of at least 0.02 % of frame each** — the largest **0.82 % of
   frame**, roughly 130×130 px at 1080p — and the largest fourteen carry the entire read. The
   character resolves into **20**. *(Compact means bbox fill ≥ 0.25 and aspect ≤ 6. Without that
   filter a smooth gouraud sky also survives a ±2 flood fill and scores as a cliff, which is exactly
   the false pass this measurement exists to catch.)*
4. **Shadow is a colour, it is the same colour on every albedo — and there are exactly two of it.**
   Rock turned from the key: `#1B2C33`, hue **198**. A shelf underside: `#202C30`, hue **195**. The
   character's armour: `#1F323C`, hue **201**. Three completely different albedos, one hue, all at
   Y 0.02–0.03. But ground *inside a cast shadow* is `#223522`, hue **120**, because it still sees
   the whole sky. Histogram every pixel at Y ≤ 0.06 in 10° hue bins and the result is **bimodal**:
   **41.3 %** of them at hue 190–210 and **29.4 %** at hue 100–140, with a trough between
   (`laws.darkPopulations.hueHistogram10Deg`). The dark end of this picture is two families, not one,
   and §3.4 is why.
5. **The world is warm and the accents are cool, at a brutal ratio.** Warm lit substance
   (hue 20–50, S ≥ 0.40, Y ≥ 0.08) is **49.76 %** of frame. Saturated cool accent
   (hue 150–200, V ≥ 0.80, S ≥ 0.25) is **0.94 %**. Fifty-three to one — and the 0.94 % is what your
   eye goes to.
6. **Distance is carried by contrast and softness, not by detail and not by desaturation.** Luminance
   spread (p95/p05) inside horizontal bands **at x ∈ [0.25, 0.85]** runs **28.2 (mid) → 12.1
   (mid-far) → 3.1 (far)** while median saturation stays between 0.40 and 0.59 the whole way. On one
   object, the lit-to-shadow ratio runs **11.96 → 2.12 → 0.94** from foreground to horizon. *(The x
   window is part of the measurement — §7.3.)*

## 2. Geometry law — form comes from the mesh, and only from the mesh

There are no normal maps in this project. There is no high-frequency surface detail. Every shape a
player reads is a shape somebody built. That makes geometry the most expensive department in the
game and the cheapest one to get wrong.

### 2.1 Flat shading is a build step, not a material flag

Two ways to get per-face normals in Three.js. **Use the first for everything static.**

```js
// (a) PREFERRED — static world geometry. Exact, derivative-free, works with any material.
//     Bake it once, at load or in the asset pipeline. Never at frame time.
geo = geo.toNonIndexed();
geo.computeVertexNormals();     // with no shared vertices this is a per-FACE normal
mat = new THREE.MeshLambertMaterial({ color, flatShading: false });

// (b) skinned / morphed meshes only, where (a) would fight the skin cache.
mat = new THREE.MeshLambertMaterial({ color, flatShading: true });
```

`flatShading: true` in Three.js r169 recovers the normal from screen-space derivatives of
`vViewPosition` (`ShaderChunk/normal_fragment_begin`). It is correct, and Lambert's lighting is
per-fragment in this version so it genuinely applies — but it costs two derivatives per pixel and it
degrades on triangles thinner than a pixel. Route (a) triples the vertex count of a mesh whose vertex
count is deliberately tiny, which is a trade we can afford everywhere.

**Do not smooth anything.** No smoothing groups, no averaged normals, no `normalScale`, no
`computeVertexNormals()` on *indexed* geometry. If a builder wants a curve, they add faces.

### 2.2 Triangle budgets and facet size, per class — **authored**

The engine budget is ≤ 1.6 M triangles, ≤ 320 draw calls, ≤ 90 programs at tier high, 1080p. Low-poly
makes that generous. **A correct Level 1 vista lands at 250 k – 600 k triangles. If you are anywhere
near 900 k you have misread the target, and the fix is fewer faces, not a lower tier.**

Facet size is stated as the **minimum short-axis length of a face, in metres, at the distance the
class is authored for**, cross-checked in pixels at 1080p with a 55° vertical FOV (1 m at distance
*d* subtends 1037/*d* px).

| class | triangles per object | min facet short axis | authored distance | px at 1080p | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **foreground cliff / spire** | 300 – 900 | **1.2 m** | 15 – 40 m | 31 – 83 | the target's largest plane is 0.82 % of frame ≈ 130×130 px at 1080p |
| **boulder / rock prop** | 40 – 160 | **0.35 m** | 6 – 15 m | 24 – 60 | smallest counted plane in §1.3 is 0.02 % of frame ≈ 20×20 px at 1080p |
| **walkable terrain shelf** | ≤ 12 000 per shelf | **3 m** | 10 – 120 m | 26 – 311 | the ground in the target is a handful of enormous planes |
| **shelf underside (fracture)** | 200 – 600 | 2 m | 20 – 200 m | 10 – 104 | reads as silhouette plus one shadow value |
| **crystal cluster** | 120 – 400 | 0.15 m | 4 – 12 m | 13 – 39 | a crystal shows 2 – 3 values, never a gradient |
| **tree / scrub blade** | 40 – 150 | 0.20 m | 4 – 20 m | 10 – 52 | scatter is silhouette only at range |
| **character (player, NPC)** | 900 – 1800 | **0.05 m** | 3 – 8 m | 6 – 17 | the target's hero resolves into **20 countable planes** at ≥ 0.01 % of frame each |
| **Ix** | 120 – 300 | 0.04 m | 2 – 5 m | 8 – 21 | §10.4 — its silhouette must not close |
| **distant city tower** | 20 – 60 | — | 400 m + | silhouette | value contrast only (§7.3) |
| **cloud slab** | 2 – 40 | — | sky | — | §6.2 |

**The rule under all of these:** a facet that never subtends 20 px on its short axis at its authored
distance is invisible geometry costing us triangles for nothing — delete it. A facet that regularly
exceeds 200 px on its short axis in the foreground is a wall, and the object needs one more cut.
Between those two numbers is the whole art.

### 2.3 Silhouette is the budget

With no surface detail, silhouette carries every read: which object it is, how far away it is,
whether you can climb it. Two hard rules:

- **Every solid gets at least one plane that catches the key and at least one fully turned from it.**
  A shape showing only lit faces reads as a flat sticker. The target's spire shows five lit values
  and one shadow value on a single mass.
- **Silhouette-defining edges are never coplanar with the camera's most common approach.** A cliff
  authored as one quad facing the player's default heading has no silhouette at all.

## 3. The lighting model that makes flat shading beautiful instead of cheap

Four lights. Anything past four is you failing to commit.

### 3.1 The rig

| light | Three.js | colour | CCT (informational only) | intensity | direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **key** — the low sun | `DirectionalLight` | `#FFE3B8` | **4505 K** | 1.00, calibrated below | elevation **+9° ± 2°**, azimuth **fixed in world** |
| **fill** — the sky | `HemisphereLight` | sky `#66B3FF`, ground `#2A1F16` | **not a blackbody** | **0.229 × key** | up |
| **bounce** — lit ground | `DirectionalLight` | `#8A5B3E` | 2833 K | 0.05 × key | up, from −40° |
| **accent** — crystal / carry | `PointLight` per cluster | `crystal.hot` `#96FDE1` | — | capped, §5.4 | local |

- **The key's colour is `#FFE3B8`, not `sky.sun`.** `sky.sun` `#FFF77D` is the sun *glow* seen
  through atmosphere — a pixel, not a light. Feeding it as the key drags lit rock toward hue 13 and
  turns sandstone into salmon. **Type the hex; never type the kelvin.** The CCT column exists so
  nobody has to guess it, not so anybody can use it.
- **The fill tint `#66B3FF` is derived**, and here is the whole derivation: take one ground plane, lit
  at `[0.545,0.760,0.575,0.790]` and inside its own cast shadow at `[0.530,0.920,0.570,0.950]`, and
  divide the shadowed linear triplet by the lit one. The albedo cancels and what is left is
  fill/(key+fill) = **(0.0852, 0.2853, 0.6351)**. Normalise to its maximum channel and that is
  `#66B3FF`. Its "CCT" computes to 52 330 K, which is meaningless — it sits nowhere near the
  Planckian locus, on purpose, and *that is why the shadows are a colour instead of a darkness.*
- **The key's azimuth is a world direction, not a camera-relative one**, and it holds one bearing for
  the whole session. A light bolted to the camera boom would swing the world's shading every time the
  player turned, and every rule in §3.4 would be meaningless.
- **Cast-shadow length is authored at 3.0 – 4.0 × object height** for legibility, which implies a sun
  nearer 16° than the 9° the sky says. The target cheats; so do we.

### 3.2 Calibration — how to know the rig is right

Two witnesses, both on the target, both re-runnable:

| witness | measured | tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| one ground plane, lit vs its own cast shadow | Y **0.1310** vs **0.0300**, ratio **4.36** | 4.36 ± 0.5 |
| one rock mass, lit plane vs plane turned from the key | Y **0.2720** vs **0.0227**, ratio **11.96** | 11.96 ± 2.0 |

Calibrate by capturing and measuring, never by dividing two light intensities.

A worked albedo, so nobody has to guess: with the key at `#FFE3B8`, `albedo = linear(brightest
measured facet) / linear(key)` gives **rock `#F5B268`** (linear 0.9131, 0.4451, 0.1390) and
**ground `#8A733E`** (linear 0.2542, 0.1730, 0.0483). *Change the key and redo the division. Do not
copy those hexes without the key they came from.*

### 3.3 How many value steps a surface may show

- **One facet: exactly one value.** Measured spread inside a face is **0.0096** of luminance.
  Anything above **0.02** across a single face means smooth normals leaked in.
- **One object: 4 – 7 distinct lit values, plus one shadow value.** The target's spire mass shows
  five lit steps (Y 0.44 / 0.31 / 0.27 / 0.24 / 0.12) and one shadow (0.0227). Fewer than four and
  the object reads as a cut-out; more than seven and the facets are too small to count, so you have
  paid for smoothness without getting it.
- **The steps sit on a cosine.** Divide each lit value by the brightest and it must land within
  **± 0.03** of a plausible N·L. If your facets do not sit on a cosine, either the normals are not
  per-face or a tone curve is doing the shading.

### 3.4 Shadowed faces stay chromatic, and they converge

This is the single most important paragraph in the document.

**Required output.** Any face turned from the key, with open sky above it and no bounce, must land at
**hue 190 – 206, S 0.33 – 0.50, Y 0.020 – 0.033**, *essentially independent of its albedo*. Three
witnesses at three albedos: rock `#1B2C33` (198), shelf underside `#202C30` (195), character armour
`#1F323C` (201).

**Cast shadow on an up-facing surface is a different animal and must not share that colour.** A plane
that still sees the whole sky keeps its own albedo and rotates only part-way: ground lit `#78632C`
(hue 43) → ground in cast shadow `#223522` (hue **120**, box `[0.530,0.920,0.570,0.950]`, Y 0.0300),
not to 198. *If a cast shadow is the same blue as a turned face, the shadow reads as a hole in the
ground.*

**The two families are measured, and §13 gates them separately.** `laws.darkPopulations` splits every
pixel at Y ≤ 0.06 by hue window:

| population | predicate | n | share of frame | median hue | median S | gated by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **turned from the key** | Y ≤ 0.06 **and hue 170–220** | 389 254 | **9.21 %** | **199.2** | 0.446 | §13 row 3 |
| **cast shadow on ground** | Y ≤ 0.06 **and hue 60–150** | 298 607 | **7.06 %** | **120.0** | 0.370 | §13 row 4 |
| *the union of the two* | Y ≤ 0.06 | 828 370 | 19.60 % | *146.7* | 0.411 | **nothing — see below** |

**Never write a gate on that third row.** Its median, 146.7, is the trough between the two modes: no
surface in the target is hue 147, and a scoreboard demanding it can only be satisfied by blue-shifting
the ground shadows — that is, by breaking this paragraph. The measured separation is
**199.2 − 120.0 = 79.2°**, and §13 rows 3 and 4 together require both families to exist, at their own
hues, at least 50° apart. *A render can no longer pass the shadow gate by crushing its greens.*

**Recipe (a) — preferred.** Inject a shadow-tint lerp with `onBeforeCompile`, after `outgoingLight`
is formed:

```glsl
// uShadowTint = linear(#1B2C33) ; uSkyVis = 1 under open sky, 0 under a lid (bake per vertex)
float keyLit = dot(geometryNormal, uKeyDir) * shadowMask;   // <= 0 means turned from the key
float w      = (1.0 - smoothstep(0.0, 0.06, keyLit)) * (1.0 - uSkyVis * 0.85);
outgoingLight = mix(outgoingLight, uShadowTint, clamp(w, 0.0, 1.0));
```

The `(1.0 - uSkyVis * 0.85)` term is what keeps a cast shadow on open ground *out* of the tint, and
therefore keeps it chromatic and albedo-coloured. Weight `w` must be blended over **≥ 0.25 s** when
it changes for a reason other than geometry (§11.3) — never switched per frame.

**Recipe (b) — tier-low fallback, with its error stated.** `HemisphereLight(sky #66B3FF,
ground #2A1F16)` at 0.229 × key, plus a per-material `emissive` of `rock.shadow × 0.5`. This lifts
lit faces by about 4 % of luminance and cools them by roughly 2° of hue. That is inside tolerance,
but it is visible on the brightest facet, which is why it is a fallback and not the target.

**Never**: multiply the albedo to make a shadow; use `#000000` anywhere; use a grey ambient; use a
violet shadow — that was the old painterly reference, and it is wrong now.

### 3.5 Ambient strategy, and the tonemap that is not there

- **`renderer.toneMapping = THREE.NoToneMapping`.** The value ladder in §1.2 is an undistorted
  cosine, which is only possible with a linear path from N·L to the final linear value. A filmic
  shoulder compresses the top of that ladder and the facets stop reading as facets. **The palette is
  the grade.** `outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, `ColorManagement.enabled = true`.
- **No ambient-occlusion pass and no screen-space GI.** Contact darkening comes from geometry: a face
  that should be dark is turned away from the key, and that is the whole mechanism. A baked
  sky-visibility scalar per vertex — used only by §3.4's `uSkyVis` — is permitted and costs nothing
  at frame time.
- **Emitters clip; they do not roll off.** Measured: **0.69 %** of frame at Y ≥ 0.90 (the sun glow,
  crystal cores, KaTeX) and **0.44 %** at Y ≤ 0.004. Budgets in §7.4.
- **Dither the final frame at 8-bit quantisation.** The target does — 378 distinct colours down a
  461-row sky column, longest constant run **4 rows** — and it is why a smooth sky ramp shows no
  visible banding.

## 4. Edges

| property | measured | rule |
|---|---|---|
| facet-edge transition width | median **2 px** at 2752 wide, p90 6 (**1.4 px at 1920**) | a light/shadow boundary is a geometric edge with antialiasing on it, never a shading falloff |
| within-facet luminance spread | **0.0096** | §3.3 |

**Antialias, do not soften.** MSAA on the main target, or FXAA where MSAA is unavailable — 1–2 px, no
more.

**No screen-space contour ink.** The old painterly bible mandated an ink line; the low-poly target has
none anywhere, and a contour pass on top of hard facets reads as a cel-shading filter sitting on a
game rather than as the game's own look. These edges are already hard; they do not need drawing
twice.

There is exactly one deliberate softness in this picture and it is depth of field (§7.3).

## 5. Material recipes, per surface class

The whole world uses **`MeshLambertMaterial`** unless a row says otherwise. Lambert is per-fragment in
Three.js r169, it has **no specular lobe at all**, and no specular lobe is exactly what this target
wants. Unlit surfaces use `MeshBasicMaterial`; the sky uses a `ShaderMaterial`. **That is the entire
list of material types in this project.**

**There is no env map in this project, on any surface, at any intensity, and therefore there is no
reason to reach for `MeshStandardMaterial`.** Earlier drafts of this section carried an escape hatch —
*"`MeshStandardMaterial` is permitted only where an env map is genuinely needed"* — and it was the one
door in the material law a PBR look could walk through, in direct contradiction of anti-pattern 18 and
of the per-row `envMap` bans below. **The hatch is deleted, not narrowed.** An env map is an image of
a reflected world; nothing in the target reflects anything. The two surfaces a builder would reach for
it on are both already answered by geometry: **crystal** shows 2–3 authored facet values plus an
emissive (§5.4), and **"metal"** is a lighter, cooler albedo and nothing else. If a future surface ever
seems to need one, that is a §13-row-22 conversation with a critic, not a material flag — and it
requires editing this paragraph first.

**Banned across the entire project, every class, no exceptions:**
`MeshStandardMaterial` · `MeshPhysicalMaterial` · `envMap` · `envMapIntensity` · `Scene.environment` ·
`PMREMGenerator` · `normalMap` · `bumpMap` · `roughnessMap` · `metalnessMap` · `displacementMap` ·
`aoMap` · `clearcoat` · `sheen` · `transmission` · `iridescence` · `anisotropy` ·
smooth or averaged vertex normals · per-pixel noise or detail textures · triplanar detail · parallax.

| class | material | key properties | emissive | banned *in addition* to the global list |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **rock / cliff / boulder** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color:` rock albedo; non-indexed per-face normals; `fog: true` | none | a Fresnel rim of any kind; any specular; a colour map |
| **terrain shelf (walkable)** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color:` ground albedo; vertex colours **only** to switch whole facets, never to gradient across one | none | vertex-colour gradients; splat maps; triplanar blending |
| **shelf underside (fracture)** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | same albedo family; faces authored turned from the key so §3.4 lands them on `rock.shadow` | teal veins are **separate strips**, §5.4 | tinting the rock to fake the veins |
| **foliage / scrub / canopy** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color: world.foliage`; `side: DoubleSide` on blades only; **alpha test, never alpha blend** | none | any bright green (§7.1); wind amplitude below 0.2 m, which turns to fizz |
| **crystal (a certainty)** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color: crystal.face`; 2 – 3 facet values authored **as separate faces** | `emissive: crystal.hot`, §5.4 | refraction; `transmission`; a Fresnel rim; a gradient across a facet. *This is the surface a builder is most likely to want an env map for. It gets faces and an emissive instead* |
| **water / a carry** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color: water.body`; flat surface facets; scrolling **UV offset of a hard-edged ramp** | `emissive: water.core` at 0.35 × crystal | normal-mapped ripples; reflection probes; refraction; foam textures |
| **metal (fittings, barge, cutters' glass)** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color`, flat | none | metalness at any value; a two-lobe specular. **There is no metal look in this target** — a "metal" object is a lighter, cooler albedo and nothing else, and that is the second surface people reach for an env map on |
| **grey (a supplied closure)** | `MeshLambertMaterial` | `color: world.grey` `#484743`, S 0.069 — the only role below S 0.10 | none | any glow; any cyan tint; **§3.4's shadow tint** — grey is the one material that does not rotate |
| **character: skin, cloth, plate** | `MeshLambertMaterial`, `flatShading: true` (skinned) | `color` per zone; hard zone boundaries cut into the mesh | none, except §10.4's accent strips | subsurface; a rim-light term; any specular; smooth normals anywhere on the body |
| **sky** | `ShaderMaterial` on a backside sphere, `depthWrite: false` | the ramp in §6.1, evaluated analytically, then dithered | — | a gradient texture; a cubemap; any noise; volumetrics |
| **cloud slab** | `MeshBasicMaterial` | flat quads on the sky sphere; **hard alpha test**; `color: cloud.slab` | — | soft alpha; billboard fade; a noise mask; volumetrics |

**Everything on this list is unlit-or-Lambert, and that is the point.** The moment one surface in
frame carries a specular highlight, the flat-shaded surfaces beside it start looking unfinished
rather than deliberate.

### 5.4 Emissive budget — the only saturated light in the world

Cyan is not a colour choice here. It is the only place the eye is told to go.

| emitter | colour | peak Y measured |
|---|---|---|
| crystal core | `crystal.hot` `#96FDE1` | 0.8217 |
| crystal turned facet | `crystal.face` `#88D1C3` | 0.5478 |
| carry surface | `water.core` `#8FFDE5` | 0.8175 |
| carry body / spill | `water.body` `#7EB9A3` | 0.4178 |
| city window strip | `crystal.hot` at 0.6 intensity | 0.042 % of frame in the target |

- **Every emitter carries a real `PointLight`** so it spills onto the ground, and the spill is the
  proof it is a light rather than a painted decal. Cap radius at **6 m**, and cap intensity so the
  spill never lifts a neighbouring rock facet above **Y 0.10** — above that the accent starts lighting
  the world instead of marking it.
- **Bloom is permitted and tightly gated.** One pass, threshold **Y 0.62**, applied *only to the
  accent class* through a mask: crystal, carry, city strips, the sun glow, KaTeX. Radius ≤ 4 % of
  frame height. **Bloom must never touch rock, ground, foliage, character or UI.** A bloom that leaks
  onto a lit rock facet destroys the flatness in a single frame.

## 6. The sky

### 6.1 The gradient — and it is a **smooth** gradient, not a posterised one

The brief's word "banded" describes what the sky *looks like*, and the banding comes from the cloud
slabs (§6.2), not from the ramp. **Measured:** a clean sky column at x = 0.63 across 461 rows carries
**378 distinct colours**, and its **longest constant run is 4 rows**. That is a smooth, dithered ramp.
A posterised sky is a different picture, and it is the wrong one.

Six stops, each the **per-row median across x ∈ [0.30, 0.70]** — median, so cloud slabs are rejected
without having to locate them:

| y (fraction of frame height) | role | hex | hue | S | Y |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | `sky.zenith` | `#568885` | 176 | 0.368 | 0.2128 |
| 0.06 | `sky.high` | `#628C84` | 169 | 0.300 | 0.2302 |
| **0.14** | **`sky.pivot`** | `#81947E` | **112** | **0.149** | 0.2735 |
| 0.22 | `sky.warm` | `#A89A72` | 44 | 0.321 | 0.3265 |
| 0.28 | `sky.low` | `#D1AB6C` | 37 | 0.483 | 0.4376 |
| 0.34 | `sky.horizon` | `#FFB260` | 31 | 0.624 | 0.5395 |

- **`sky.pivot` is the saturation minimum of the entire column, and it is not optional.** A
  zenith→horizon ramp that never drops below **S 0.16** reads instantly as a two-colour lerp. The
  ramp has to pass through a near-neutral on its way from teal to ochre.
- **The sky also runs horizontally.** On the horizon row, the sun end of frame is `#FFF77D`
  (Y **0.8926**) and the far end is `#EF9F5B` (Y **0.4390**) — a **2.03 : 1** gradient across the
  width of the picture, at one altitude. A sky authored as a function of altitude alone loses half
  the drama and all of the direction.
- **Evaluate the ramp analytically in the shader and dither the result.** Never sample a gradient
  texture: at 8 bits, a 1536-tall ramp between two of these stops bands visibly, and dither is exactly
  how the target avoids it.
- **The sun is a glow, not a disc.** `sky.sun` `#FFF77D`, Y 0.8926 — no hard rim, no ghosts, no
  anamorphic streak, no lens dirt.

### 6.2 Cloud slabs — this is where "banded" comes from

**Measured:** cloud pixels (hue 20–60, S ≥ 0.35, Y ≥ 0.45, above y = 0.22) are **4.13 % of frame**,
median `#F7CA84`, hue 37, S 0.466, Y **0.6368**. Two slab-against-its-own-sky pairs, each a box inside
a slab against clean sky directly above it at the same x:

| slab | sky above it | luminance ratio | Δhue |
|---|---|---|---|
| `#FFDB8E` Y 0.7388, hue 41 | `#8FAC92` Y 0.3742, hue 126 | **1.97** | **85°** |
| `#FECC84` Y 0.6592, hue 35 | `#6A8D80` Y 0.2367, hue 158 | **2.78** | **122°** |

The specification, authored from that measurement:

- **Slabs are warm; the sky at their altitude is cool.** Δhue **85 – 122°**, and the slab sits at
  **2.0 – 2.8 × the local sky luminance**. That contrast is the whole effect.
- **Two values per slab, never three:** a lit body and one darker step along its underside. No
  gradient, no soft edge, no feather.
- **Edges are stair-stepped, axis-aligned and hard.** The target's slabs are built from rectangular
  blocks with visible right-angle steps. Author them as a small number of quads on the sky sphere with
  **alpha test**, never alpha blend. A soft-edged cloud in this world reads as a bug. Measured edge
  transition on a slab silhouette: median **5 px at 2752 (3.49 px at 1920)**, p90 6 — a hard shape
  carrying a little more antialiasing than foreground rock (2 px) because it is far away. Anything
  above 4 px at 1920 is a feathered cloud and it is wrong.
- **Count and placement — authored.** 3 – 6 slab groups in a hero framing, all confined to
  y ∈ [0.02, 0.22], each 8 – 25 % of frame width, none crossing the frame centre horizontally, none
  touching the horizon. Big, simple, confident shapes. **Fewer, larger slabs beat more, smaller ones
  every time.**
- Slabs drift laterally at **≤ 0.4 % of frame width per second**. Faster reads as a screen saver;
  slower is not worth the draw call.

## 7. Colour law

### 7.1 The warm / cool split

| class | gate | measured share | allowed | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **warm lit substance** | hue 20–50, S ≥ 0.40, Y ≥ 0.08 | **49.76 %** | 0.35 – 0.60 | rock and ground are the mass of the picture |
| **cool shadow** | hue 170–220, Y ≤ 0.06 | **9.21 %** | 0.05 – 0.16 | near zero means the render has no shadow direction; over 0.16 and the frame has gone gloomy. **This is §13 row 3's population, hue window included** |
| **cast shadow on ground** | hue 60–150, Y ≤ 0.06 | **7.06 %** | ≥ 0.02 | the other half of §3.4, and **§13 row 4's population**. It is a *subset* of the green row below |
| **saturated cool accent** | hue 150–200, V ≥ 0.80, S ≥ 0.25 | **0.94 %** | **0.4 – 1.8 %** | §7.2 |
| **green** | hue 60–150, S ≥ 0.25 | **9.70 %** | 0.04 – 0.16 | almost all of it is dark ground cover and cast shadow — 7.06 of those 9.70 points are the row above |
| **near-neutral** | S ≤ 0.08 | **1.01 %** | ≤ 0.03 | the only neutral things in frame are UI bevels, KaTeX and grey |

**Warm : cool accent is 53 : 1 by area.** The rock is the mass; the cyan is the meaning.

**Green is a value, not a colour.** Measured foliage is `#203120` at **Y 0.026, S 0.347**; the
brightest foliage anywhere in the target is `#585939` at **Y 0.095**. Foliage may never exceed
**S 0.45** or **Y 0.22**. Above either it starts reading as a UI success state rather than a plant —
which is precisely why `ui.success` is constructed at S 0.762, well clear of it.

### 7.2 The cyan rule

> **Saturated cyan appears on crystal and on water. Nothing else. Ever.**

- The gate is **hue 150–200, V ≥ 0.80, S ≥ 0.25**, and the frame budget is **0.4 % – 1.8 %**
  (measured 0.94 %). Below 0.4 % the world stops having a subject; above 1.8 % the accent stops being
  an accent.
- In the target the split is roughly: carry/river **0.61 %**, near crystal **0.25 %**, far crystal
  **0.22 %**, city window strips **0.04 %**. *Those sampling boxes overlap, so they sum above the
  frame-wide total; the frame-wide **0.94 %** is the number that governs.*
- **There is no cyan in the sky.** Cyan inside the gate above y = 0.30 is **0.01 %** of frame — that
  is, none. The sky is teal at low saturation (`sky.zenith` S 0.368, V 0.533) and never enters the
  accent gate. A sky that does has stolen the accent's job.
- **The one permitted extension** is an emissive strip on built architecture — window lines on a
  city, inlays on a claim socket — because in this world those *are* live mathematics. They count
  against the 1.8 % like everything else.
- **Cool hue is shared with shadow, and they are separated by value, not hue.** `rock.shadow` sits at
  hue 198 and `crystal.hot` at hue 164 — but the real separator is Y **0.0227** against **0.8217**, a
  factor of 36. This is elegant and it is fragile: lift the shadows and the accent stops being an
  accent.

### 7.3 Depth law — distance is contrast and softness, never detail

**The predicate is a rectangle, and it has two axes.** Every row below is the percentiles of the
pixels at `y ∈ [y0, y1)` **and `x ∈ [0.25, 0.85)`** — the same window the sampler uses
(`laws.depthBands[].x`, `laws.depthBandsMethod`) and the same window `--check` row 10 uses. **The x
window is not a detail, it is half the measurement:** the left quarter of the frame is the foreground
spire and the right sixth is a near floating shelf, and both of them stand *inside rows that belong to
far bands*. Sample full width instead and the answers move a long way — far **8.79**, mid-far
**25.27**, mid **31.99**, near **22.84**, far median S **0.575** — which is a true measurement of a
different question, and not this table.

| band | y | x | n | Y p05 | Y p50 | Y p95 | **p95/p05** | median S | edge width @2752 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| near | 0.64 – 0.78 | 0.25 – 0.85 | 354 965 | 0.0161 | 0.1346 | 0.3918 | **24.3** | 0.525 | **2 px** |
| mid | 0.52 – 0.64 | 0.25 – 0.85 | 303 784 | 0.0278 | 0.2314 | 0.7845 | **28.2** | 0.395 | **5 px** |
| mid-far | 0.42 – 0.52 | 0.25 – 0.85 | 254 254 | 0.0693 | 0.4225 | 0.8352 | **12.1** | 0.547 | **6 px** |
| far | 0.36 – 0.42 | 0.25 – 0.85 | 151 892 | 0.2352 | 0.5202 | 0.7248 | **3.1** | 0.592 | **7 px** |

And the same law on one object rather than on a band — the lit-to-shadow ratio of a single mass at
three depths: **11.96 (foreground) → 2.12 (mid) → 0.94 (horizon).**

Three consequences, all checkable:

1. **Facet contrast collapses with distance; saturation does not.** Median saturation stays between
   0.40 and 0.59 across the whole depth range. **Do not implement aerial perspective as
   desaturation.** Implement it as a lerp of the *lit* value toward `sky.horizon` `#FFB260` while the
   *shadow* value rises to meet it, so the two converge.
2. **The horizon is where a lit face and a shadowed face become the same colour.** Measured at
   y ≈ 0.375: a lit distant plane is `#FEAD61` (Y 0.5182) and a turned one is `#FDB664` (Y 0.5526) —
   a ratio of **0.94**. Distant ground *disappears into the sky*, and that is correct behaviour.
3. **Distant silhouettes still hold.** Two far city towers against the sky immediately beside them:
   `#A6865B` Y 0.2591 against `#FFE373` Y 0.7744, ratio **0.33**; and `#927B5B` Y 0.2103 against
   `#F7A35B` Y 0.4672, ratio **0.45**. So distant ground planes vanish and verticals survive at
   **0.33 – 0.45 × the sky beside them**. That asymmetry is what makes a horizon pose a question.

**Depth of field is far-only, and it is measured.** Median hard-edge width runs **2 px (near) → 5
(mid) → 6 (mid-far) → 7 (far)** at 2752 wide. The near field is *sharp* — there is no near blur at
all. Implement as a single-radius far-field blur keyed to depth, ramping in past roughly 25 m, capped
at **7 px at 2752 (≈ 5 px at 1920)**. Nothing in the foreground is ever softened, because a soft
foreground on a low-poly game reads as a broken renderer.

### 7.4 Exposure budget

| gate | measured | allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Y ≥ 0.90 | **0.69 %** | 0.2 – 2.0 % |
| pure `#FFFFFF` | **0.11 %** | ≤ 0.4 % |
| Y ≤ 0.004 | **0.44 %** | ≤ 1.2 % |

Frame percentiles for reference: p01 0.0067 · p05 0.0197 · p25 0.1027 · **p50 0.2321** · p75 0.3982 ·
p95 0.7220 · p99 0.8797. **The median frame is dark.** A render whose p50 has climbed above about
0.32 has lost the dusk.

## 8. The mathematics, in world space

> **White is the statement. It floats. There is nothing behind it.**

- **`math.glyph` is `#FFFFFF`, Y = 1.000, and it is measured that way.** 0.11 % of the target's
  pixels are pure white, and they are the equation, the graph and the sun's hottest core.
- **No panel. No glass. No frame. No corner brackets. No scrim. No drop shadow. No outline.**
  Measured across a glyph edge, the transition from `#FFFFFF` to the backdrop completes in **1–2 px**
  with nothing in between. Every one of those chrome elements was in the old painterly bible; every
  one of them is now banned.
- **Stroke weight is what carries it.** Pure-white horizontal runs inside the equation: median
  **6 px** at 2752 (**4.2 px at 1920**), p90 9. Author world-space KaTeX at a size that lands the
  *median stroke* at **3.5 – 5 px at 1080p**, scaling with drawing-buffer height, never with a fixed
  px value.
- **The graph is drawn in the same white, as axis-aligned stair steps**, with tick marks — a
  deliberately quantised line, not a smooth one. Plotted curves follow the same rule.

### 8.1 How it stays legible against a bright sky — with the numbers

| measurement | value |
|---|---|
| backdrop luminance behind the target's equation, median | **0.4618** |
| the same backdrop, p99 | 0.7938 |
| contrast at the median backdrop | **2.17 : 1** |
| contrast at the worst local backdrop | 1.26 : 1 |
| the sky at the sun end of the horizon | Y **0.8926** |

That worst case, 1.26 : 1, is not legible — and the target avoids it by **placement**: the equation
hangs over the mid-sky, not over the sun. Our camera moves, so placement cannot be trusted, and this
is the rule that replaces it:

> **The equation layer measures the luminance of what is behind its glyph mask. When the local median
> rises above Y 0.55 it cross-fades in a hard 2 px (at 1080p) outline in `ui.outline` `#080E0B` over
> 0.20 s. Below Y 0.45 the outline is fully out; between them it ramps, with 0.10 of luminance of
> hysteresis so it cannot flicker as the camera pans.** No panel, no scrim, no glow, ever.

An outline in the world's own near-black is the only chrome this project permits on mathematics, it
exists only when it is needed, and it keeps the bare-white-floating-in-air read intact everywhere
else.

## 10. What is in front of the camera

`design/world.md` is the other binding document, and where it and the target disagree, **world.md
wins on content and the target wins on look.** Four places where that matters, restated in low-poly
terms.

### 10.1 There is no ground underneath

`world.md` §2.3: *"a piece that puts a distant valley floor under the clouds has broken the premise."*
The midground is **leaves** — floating shelves, flat on top because that was the surface, ragged
underneath because that is a fracture.

- **`W1`** — a Level-1 hero framing must contain **at least two separate regions of sky *below* the
  horizon line**, each ≥ 1 % of frame area, seen between or under shelves.
- **`W2`** — trace the lowest visible surface in any downward sightline: it ends in sky or in a
  shelf's underside, **never in terrain.** A gap between two shelves gets *brighter* as it goes down,
  because there is sky down there — the exact opposite of a canyon, and it sells the premise in one
  frame.
- A shelf underside is `rock.shadow` family (it is a face turned from the key, so §3.4 puts it there
  automatically) with **teal veins as separate emissive strips**, never as a tint on the rock. Vein
  pixels count against §7.2's accent budget like everything else.

### 10.2 Three materials, and each one means something

| material | what it *is*, diegetically | roles | frame budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **warm stone** | what cooled — a claim that closed and settled | `rock.*`, `ground.*` | 0.35 – 0.60 |
| **teal resonance** | what has not resolved yet — an open socket, a live claim, a carry | `crystal.*`, `water.*` | **0.004 – 0.018** |
| **grey** | what was answered instead of solved | `world.grey` `#484743`, `world.grey.deep` `#292826` | 0.02 – 0.08 |

`world.grey` is **constructed, not sampled** — the target contains no grey material — and the
construction is printed in `palette.json`: hue = hue(`ground.lit`), S = 0.08, V solved so its
luminance is the geometric mean of lit and shadowed ground, **√(0.1310 × 0.0300) = 0.0629**. It is the
only role in the palette below S 0.10, and that is what makes "approximate" legible as a material at
a glance.

**Grey does not take §3.4's shadow rotation** (Δhue 0, S × 0.84, Y × 0.34), because a hue rotation is
what a surface does when a coloured world is lighting it, and grey reads as a surface the world has
partly stopped talking to. A blue shadow on grey turns it back into rock, and it is the fastest way
to lose the material. Grey also sags: a greyed span is the right shape and the wrong length, so it is
authored with a visible droop of **1.5 – 3 % of its own span at mid-length**, with props under it —
and the props are not grey; the thing they hold up is.

**When a claim closes, the light has to leave.** `world.md` §4.2: *snapping* — light first, then
stone, about eight frames. The emitted object's emissive ramps to **zero** across the snap, and what
stands afterwards is `rock.*` with no glow, no bloom and no cyan edge. A closed span that keeps a cyan
rim is a lie about game state rendered in light, and it is worse than a wrong colour.

### 10.3 The frame is never empty, and it is never all human

`world.md` §0. **`W3`** — in a Level-1 hero framing, at least three non-player presences must resolve
at 128 px, and **at least one must be non-human in silhouette.** Low-poly makes this cheaper, not
harder: a non-human read needs only two of these four levers, and all four are geometry.

1. **A body axis that is not vertical** — horizontal, coiled, arched or lying.
2. **A limb count that is not two-and-two**, or one that changes.
3. **A scale that is not 1–2 m** — cart-sized, house-sized, or six hundred metres long and only ever
   visible as two arches.
4. **A grammatical number the frame can show** — two identical silhouettes drinking in two places, at
   the same moment, with one gait.

No faces on the unnameables — *"they never turn to face you, because there is no facing."* And **the
Bollard and the Second Lip may never both be in one framing**: if a camera can hold both arches, the
level is laid out wrong, and that is a P09 bug this document is allowed to report.

### 10.4 The player, and Ix

**The player is issued kit, not heroic plate.** Measured on the target's character:

| zone | hex | Y | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| armour, shadow side | `#1F323C` (hue 201) | 0.0290 | **the player is mostly this colour** — the same family as `rock.shadow` |
| armour, key-lit edge | `#F3B16D` (hue 30) | 0.5160 | one or two facets on the sun side |
| hair / leather | `#442611` | 0.0266 | faceted, three values, near-black core |
| straps, gloves, soles | `#111416` | 0.0068 | cool near-black, never `#000000` |

That single warm rim strip against a blue-grey body is what separates a character from warm ground at
any distance. **Never desaturate the character to grey**, never give it a specular, never give it a
rim-light term — the rim in the target is *geometry catching the key*, not a shader trick.

The can hangs at one hip and is the one permitted violation of the silhouette's taper: asymmetric, one
side only, readable at 128 px as a mass the shoulder line does not explain. A hero silhouette with no
can is a hero; this character is a courier.

**Ix's silhouette must not close.** No closed outline, no head, no wings, no face, no eyes: an open
bracket and a point that never settled, with exactly four lights — and the count is the truth, owned
by the mastery engine and merely rendered here. Test at 128 px, desaturated; if a stranger names an
animal, the model is wrong. In low-poly that is a *harder* discipline, not an easier one, because
faceted blobs read as creatures by default.

## 11. Under motion — **authored**, because a still cannot be measured for any of it

`quality-bar.md` §1 ranks this above everything else in this file: *"Readability under motion beats
detail at rest. If it turns to noise while running, it is wrong."* Flat shading has one specific
failure mode under motion, and it is not the one PBR has.

1. **Facet flicker.** A hard geometric light/shadow boundary on a small face flips value in one frame
   when the camera moves a pixel. That is why §2.2 puts a **20 px minimum** on facet short axis: a
   facet smaller than that is a per-frame coin flip.
2. **Shadow-map crawl.** Long cast shadows (§3.1) are where map artefacts show first. Cascade splits
   at **12 / 40 / 140 m**, texel-snapped to world space so the shadow does not swim as the camera
   translates.
3. **Shadow-family blending.** §3.4's weight `w` ramps over **≥ 0.25 s** when it changes for a reason
   other than geometry — an emitter coming into range, a cascade switch. A hard switch is a visible
   colour pop.
4. **Emitter energy.** An emitter's screen energy may change by no more than **3 % per fixed step**.
   Pulsing crystal is charming; strobing crystal is a bug.
5. **DOF stability.** The far-blur radius may move by no more than **0.5 px per fixed step**. A DOF
   that chases the depth buffer breathes.
6. **The budget.** With the camera static and the simulation advanced one fixed step, **no more than
   0.2 % of pixels may change by more than 0.05 of luminance.** Sparkling facets blow this, and
   nothing in §1–§10 can see it.

## 12. Anti-patterns

### 12.1 What would make this read as a cheap WebGL demo

1. **Smooth vertex normals anywhere.** The single fastest way to lose.
2. **A specular highlight on rock.** There is no specular anywhere in the target.
3. **A Fresnel rim on anything.** Reads as wet plastic instantly.
4. **A gradient across a single facet.** Measured spread inside a face is 0.0096; above 0.02 means
   smooth normals leaked in.
5. **Grey or black shadows.** A turned face is hue 190–206 at S 0.33–0.50; ground in a cast shadow is
   hue 100–140. A neutral shadow is a renderer with no art direction — and **one** shadow colour for
   both cases is a renderer with half of one (§3.4).
6. **A filmic tonemap.** It compresses the value ladder and the facets stop reading as facets.
7. **A default `MeshStandardMaterial` at roughness 0.5.** Half the WebGL on the internet.
8. **A texture on anything.** No albedo maps, no noise, no detail, no splat.
9. **Uniform triangle density.** Low-poly is *authored* density: big planes where the form is simple,
   more cuts where it turns.
10. **A bloom that touches everything.** Threshold Y 0.62 and mask to the accent class, or it eats the
    picture.
11. **A skybox that is a lerp between two colours.** It must pass through `sky.pivot` at S 0.149.
12. **Banding in the sky**, from sampling a gradient texture instead of evaluating and dithering.
13. **Antialiasing turned off "to look retro".** The target antialiases at 1–2 px. Jaggies are not the
    style; hard *edges* are.
14. **A screen-space contour ink pass.** The target has none, and on hard facets it reads as a filter
    on top of a game rather than as the game.
15. **Emissive without a light.** An emitter with no spill is a painted decal.
16. **Z-fighting on coplanar shelves.** With this few triangles there is no excuse.

### 12.2 What would make this read as the wrong genre

17. **Smooth organic blobs** — subdivided rocks, rounded terrain, capsule creatures. A different game,
    and a much worse one.
18. **PBR sheen** — metalness, clearcoat, env maps, anything that says "material scan". **There is no
    surface anywhere in this project that takes an env map**, and §5 no longer carries an exception
    for one; `MeshStandardMaterial`, `Scene.environment` and `PMREMGenerator` are on the global ban
    list beside it. Nothing in the target reflects anything.
19. **Soft gradients everywhere** — a sky that fades into terrain that fades into fog. This world is
    made of *steps*.
20. **Fog soup.** Distance is contrast collapse plus a far-only blur, not a grey veil; median
    saturation stays 0.40–0.59 all the way to the horizon.
21. **Glass UI** — frosted panels, blur-behind, rounded corners, drop shadows, glow. All banned.
22. **A hologram panel behind the mathematics.** Banned in §8, and it was in the old bible.
23. **Purple or violet shadows.** Inherited from the painterly reference. Shadow here is hue 198,
    blue.
24. **A saturated green world.** Foliage is Y ≤ 0.22, S ≤ 0.45, and it is nearly all shadow.
25. **Cyan as a general sci-fi tint** — on rock, on UI chrome, on the sky, on rim lights. Cyan means
    live mathematics and nothing else.
26. **A distant valley floor under the clouds.** Breaks the premise, not just the art direction
    (§10.1).
27. **A dark, gritty grade.** The tone is optimistic sci-fi wonder: the frame is warm, the sky is
    generous, and the darkest thing in it is a shadow with a hue.

## 13. How to tell if we lost

A hostile critic puts our capture beside `reference/target-lowpoly.png` and looks for these, in this
order. Each is a *symptom* you can see, with a *measurement* behind it.

**Rows marked ✅ are executed by `node review/p02-r5-sample.mjs --check <shot.png>` — twelve of them.**
Everything else is an eye check on a real capture, and both kinds are binding.

> **Rows 3 and 4 are one gate in two halves, and they are deliberately impossible to cheat.** §3.4
> requires the dark end of the frame to be *bimodal* — turned faces at hue ~199, cast shadow on ground
> at hue ~120 — so a single median over all dark pixels lands at 147, a hue nothing in the target is.
> The old row 3 was written that way and **failed on `target-lowpoly.png` itself**, and the only way to
> pass it was to blue-shift or desaturate the ground shadows, i.e. to break §3.4 and silently fail
> row 4. Row 3 now carries §7.1's own hue window plus a share floor so it cannot be satisfied by forty
> blue pixels; row 4 measures the other mode and their separation, so it cannot be satisfied by
> crushing the greens. **You have to build both families to score either row.**

| # | symptom | number that proves it | pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ✅ | the rock looks *soft* — you cannot count the planes | compact flat regions at tol 2/255 (bbox fill ≥ 0.25, aspect ≤ 6) | ≥ 25 of ≥ 0.02 % of frame |
| 2 ✅ | edges are mush, or there are no hard edges at all | median width of a hard luminance step in the foreground, and how many exist | ≤ 3 px at 1920, and > 20 found |

| 5 ✅ | the picture has no subject | frame share at hue 150–200, V ≥ 0.80, S ≥ 0.25 | 0.4 – 1.8 % |
| 6 | cyan has leaked | any pixel in that gate on rock, sky, UI chrome or a rim | zero |

| 8 ✅ | the sky bands | longest run of one exact colour down a sky column | ≤ 6 rows |
| 9 | the clouds are soft | edge transition width on a cloud silhouette | ≤ 4 px at 1920 (target measures 3.49) |

| 11 ✅ | aerial perspective was done as desaturation | median S in the far band, same x window | ≥ 0.45 |

**Rows 1–21 are arithmetic. Row 22 is the job.** The twelve ✅ rows score **12 / 12 on
`reference/target-lowpoly.png`** and **2 / 12 on the current build** — that is the only reason to
trust either number. Matching the target is the floor, not the ceiling:
the target is one frame of one camera with nothing moving in it, and everything we have that it does
not — weather crossing the shelves, carrywrens arriving fractionally before their own noise, a bridge
snapping into existence out of light that cools into stone — is where we are supposed to win.

## 13.1 The palette rename, and the seam that keeps the build up

Round 5 renamed every role in `design/palette.json`. `app/src` — `Lighting.js`, `Materials.js`,
`Sky.js`, `Scatter.js`, `Terrain.js`, `GradePass.js`, all owned by other pieces — still asks for the
old names, and one of them dereferenced a role directly: `palette.roles["resonance.core"].hex` threw
during module evaluation and took **the entire boot** down with it. Every capture in the project was
unreviewable for as long as that lasted.

The fix lives inside this piece's own files, because the blast radius came from this piece's own file:

- **`palette.deprecatedRoles`** lists all 31 old names still in use, and each one is also
  **materialised inside `roles`** carrying the *new measured colour* plus a `deprecated: { use, why }`
  field. Nothing throws, and nothing gets a stale painterly colour — `resonance.core` now resolves to
  `crystal.hot` `#96FDE1`, not to the old `#2FE3D6`.
- **`palette.removedRoles`** names four roles that have **no replacement on purpose**: `holo.veil`,
  `holo.stroke`, `aurora.teal`, `aurora.violet`. The surfaces they described are banned by the target
  (§8, §12.2). Asking for one should fail loudly, and `Materials.roleHex` already paints debug magenta
  and warns.
- **`palette.motion`** is authored here rather than left to `core/paletteCompat.js`, because
  `Lighting.js` needs `timeOfDay.lethisVariability` and the key's elevation and variability envelope
  are art direction, not gameplay. §3.1 and §11 are its reasoning.

**This is a seam, not a palette. When the owning pieces move to the new names, delete it.**
