Why a guesser cannot get there (band 3 numbers)
  1. All-wrong fixed point of the BKT recursion: P(known) settles at 0.2296.
     The learn rate keeps a floor under P; that floor is far below the 0.95 gate.
  2. From that floor, consecutive lucky guesses drive P to: 0.8315 -> 0.9873 -> 0.9992 -> 0.9999
     so 2 consecutive lucky guesses would clear M1 on their own — probability 0.07^2 = 4.90e-3 per attempt window.
  3. M2 forces >= 6 scored opportunities with >= 3 at band, so the run has to happen late in a long window,
     and M3 forces >= 2 distinct item forms, so one exploitable interface is not enough.
  4. M4 then demands 3/4 on an unhinted, uniformly sampled check at least 12 h later: probability 0.0013.
  5. Bound on certifying ONE knowledge point by guessing, in three currencies:
       model's own guess parameter 0.0700:  0.07^2 x 0.0013 = 6.37e-6
       TRUE blind rate of the best scored form (0.0300):  9.50e-8   <- the honest number
       TRUE blind rate of a yes/no item (0.5000):  7.81e-2   <- what it would be if judge2 were scored
     The third row is four orders of magnitude worse than the first. It is not a hypothetical:
     it is what the gate is worth on any node whose items are served as closed questions, and it
     is why model.forms.scored is a hard list rather than guidance. Measured end to end in
     review/p03/mastery-sim.mjs, which serves a bot judge2 items on purpose.
     Expected knowledge points certified across all 32 of Level 1, honest currency: 3.04e-6.
  6. And a certification that does slip through is revoked by the spaced review at +1 d,
     which the same guesser passes with probability 0.0591 at best.

  Note: this bound assumes the guesser is patient. The latency floor (900 ms + 120 ms/token)
  removes upward credit from fast correct responses entirely, so mashing is strictly worse than patient guessing.
  And the learn rate is the other half of the floor: at 0.2000 the all-wrong fixed point sits at 0.2296,
  so the relearn boost is capped at 0.32 and withheld until a node has genuinely been
  mastered once (spacing.relearnRequiresPriorMastery), or the floor rises far enough that a single
  lucky answer clears M1 on its own.
