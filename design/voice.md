# The voice bible

Binding. `design/world.md` says what is true; this file says how it is said. Every player-visible
string in the product — dialogue, barks, HUD verbs, menu labels, error text, achievement names —
obeys this document.

---

## 0. The blend, and who carries it

Four references, four dials. The trick is that **each dial is carried by a different speaker**, so
the blend is structural rather than mushy. Never try to make one line do all four.

| Reference | What we take | Who carries it | Lines here |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Fourth Wing* | A bond you earn; stakes that are personal; a rite that changes you — **and the thing a bond needs in order to mean anything, which is a cohort.** | **Ix** (25), and **Dace** (8): your rank, your circuit, your age, half a leaf ahead of you all level | **33** |
| *Red Rising* | Hierarchy, ascent, a protagonist who out-thinks the system. | **Sennar** (21); **Sixth Vey** (3), who does not stop; and **Dace** (8), who *is* the system — the free value is a faster ladder and he is climbing it | **32** |
| *Hitchhiker's* | The universe as a slightly badly-run institution. Dry. Gleeful. | **Adjuster Camber** (17) and the signage (20) | **37** |
| *Valerian* | Overwhelming, generous, colourful strangeness. A thousand cultures in one frame — **at body plans, scales and grammatical numbers you cannot name, and nobody stops to explain any of them.** | **Everything crossing the leaf** — seven walk-on registers (35, §2b), one of which is not a person; ambient discovery (55), signage (20), plaques (24) | **134**, *and the count is not the evidence* — see the gate below |

**254 shippable strings**: 238 in the fenced blocks below, plus the **16 `fail.*` rows in §4's
misconception table**, which are strings that ship and are counted here even though they live in a
table rather than a fence. **Two of the numbers above deliberately share.** The signage counts for
both Hitchhiker's and Valerian, because a sign is an institution being funny at a landscape's
expense. Dace counts for both Fourth Wing and Red Rising, and that is his whole job: he is the cohort
*and* he is the proof that the system rewards the shortcut. **Every number in this table is
regenerated by `review/p01/count-lines.mjs`, never estimated. Change a string, rerun it, fix the
row.**

**The Valerian dial is the one that cannot be defended with a number, and it is the one most likely
to be.** A tone dial with a table row and no lines is not a tone dial — and a tone dial with a
hundred and thirty lines of *conceptual* strangeness is not one either. Valerian's actual signature
is that you cannot identify most of what is in frame: a river running uphill is a strange idea, but
a river running uphill described by six guilds of humans is Ankh-Morpork with better weather. So the
count is only half the row, and this is the other half:

> **The Valerian gate.** At least **four** of the presences in `world.md` §3's Level 1 manifest must
> be **unnameable in one word** — one at a scale that cannot be got into a single shot, one that is
> unmistakably somebody at work and not shaped like a person, and one that is grammatically plural.
> And at least **one walk-on register must belong to something that could not speak it if it had a
> human body** (`walk.rate.*`). Both halves are checked in `review/p01/validate-canon.mjs`; the line
> count is checked in `count-lines.mjs`; **neither script's pass is the other one's evidence.**

**Fourth Wing and Red Rising are the dials that go quiet first**, because they live in named
characters and named characters are expensive. The tell that one has gone ornamental is structural,
not numeric: *Fourth Wing without a cohort is just a pet, and Red Rising without a system to
out-think is just a promotion.* If a future revision deletes Dace, both rows collapse to decoration
and the revision is wrong.

Standing budget, so it cannot quietly erode again: **the ambient + signage + walk-on + plaque
registers must together carry more lines than any single named speaker.** Currently 137 against 25.
If a future revision has Ix at forty lines and the world at twelve, the frame has gone empty. *This
budget is necessary and not sufficient — it catches an empty frame and it cannot catch a frame full
of humans. The gate above is what catches that one.*

And the fifth thing, which is not a reference and is not optional:

**Optimism.** This world is broken and the people in it are *interested*. Nobody in the Margin is
tired of it. No line in this product may imply that learning is a burden, that the player is behind,
or that the situation is hopeless. Melancholy premise, delighted tone.

## 1. The one rule

> **The game never sounds like a classroom.**

Not "sounds like a fun classroom." Not "sounds like a classroom with a spaceship." Never a
classroom. The Margin has no schools, no examiners, no grades and no word for mathematics, and
every string must be written by somebody who has genuinely forgotten those things exist.

### Banned vocabulary, with the in-world word to use instead

Hard ban. These words do not appear in any player-visible string, in any locale.

| Never | Say |
|---|---|
| problem, question, exercise | **claim** |
| answer, solution | **value**, or *close it* |
| correct / incorrect, right / wrong | **it stands** / **it falls**, **true** / **false** |
| try again | *(nothing — the claim is still standing open; that is the message)* |
| good job, well done, nice work, great! | a **Rung**, or a specific observation, or an object appearing |
| lesson, tutorial, practice, study, homework, drill | **the working**, **the field**, **a run** |
| learn, teach, master (verb), skill, level up | **close**, **set**, **stand**, **Rung** |
| score, points, XP, stars, streak, combo | **certainties**, **the Quorum**, **Ix's digits** |
| hint, help, show solution *(as a UI affordance label)* | **the Sufficiency offers** |
| student, player, user | **Runner**, or the character's Rung |
| math, mathematics, equation, algebra | **claim** — and mostly, say nothing; look at the thing |
| inequality | **a threshold**, or **the mark** |
| greater than, less than, at least, at most *(as a label)* | **it admits** / **it refuses**, and the mark it does it by |
| expression | **a load** — the thing standing on one pan |
| variable | **a socket** (the place) or **an unknown** (the thing that climbs in) |
| substitute *(verb)* | **seat** — you seat a value into every socket the name opened |
| distribute, expand *(as an instruction)* | **open a bundle** |
| simplify, collect | **gather** |
| you should, remember to, don't forget | *(nothing — state the consequence, never the procedure)* |

Note the last row. **We do not name the subject.** The player is doing algebra for twenty minutes
and the product never once says the word. That is not coyness; it is the world being consistent.

### Seven rules for never sounding like a classroom

1. **Never explain the rule you just used.** The world states consequences, never procedures. If the
   player carried a term across the Sill and it inverted, the game shows the inversion and says
   nothing. The rule is learned by having happened to you.
2. **No second-person instruction voice.** No "now you'll learn to…", no "let's try…", no "great,
   now do it yourself." A character may give an *order* (Sennar does, constantly). Nothing may give
   a *lesson*.
3. **Praise is never generic and never about effort.** It is rank, a specific observation, or a
   thing physically appearing in the world. "Good job" is three words that undo everything.
4. **Failure text contains no evaluation of the player.** Only a description of the claim. The claim
   fell; the claim leans; one side moved. Nobody in the Margin has an opinion about you.
5. **No meta-commentary on learning.** Never "you're getting better at this," never "you've mastered
   X," never a progress congratulation, and never a reassurance *about not knowing* — that is a
   teacher's sentence no matter whose mouth it is in. Ix's lights and your Rung *show* it. Saying it
   out loud turns the world back into a school in one sentence.
6. **No difficulty apology and no difficulty gloating.** Never "this one's tricky!" and never
   "easy, right?" Both tell the player how to feel about a thing they can see.
7. **The system voice never exclaims.** Exclamation marks belong to Ix and to nobody else in the
   product. That is the entire emotional budget — see §2, where it is spent down to the mark.

## 2. The named speakers

*Three carry the dials. A fourth — **Dace**, §2c — has eight lines and exists so that the player has
somebody their own age to lose to.*

### IX — the stray unknown *(carries: the bond)*

**Wants:** to be definite. To be *something*, specifically, even briefly, even dangerously.
**Fears:** being rounded; and separately, and more, never having been anything at all.
**Never:** instructs, praises generically, explains a rule, tells the player what to press,
comforts the player about not knowing. Ix has never been to a school and does not know the shape of
that sentence.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Short. Median 6 words. Hard cap **9 words** for a HUD bark. |
| Rhythm | Fragments. Restarts. Interrupts itself when excited, which is most of the time. |
| Numbers | Used as **adjectives** and as feelings. "A very four sort of door." "That's a loud number." |
| Names | Terrible at them. Calls Sennar "the old Seventh." Calls Camber "the polite one." Never gets Vantis wrong, because Ix is frightened of Vantis. |
| Punctuation | Owns every exclamation mark in the product. Uses question marks constantly. |
| Never says | "should", "wrong", "correct", "try", any instruction, any word from the banned list. |
| Emotional tell | When Ix is scared, the sentences get **longer and more polite**. That is the only time. |

**The exclamation budget, spent.** There are **exactly three exclamation marks in Level 1**, all
Ix's, and here they are: `ix.first.solve.01` (the first time Ix is a number), `ix.bark.walker.01`
(seeing a Sixth Rung walk on a drawn line), `ix.mastery.digit.01` (getting the three back). Nothing
else in the product exclaims, in any locale, including Ix's other lines. Three is the budget because
three is what makes each one land.

**Sample lines**

ix.bark.verse.01        She hasn't stopped singing. Not once. Not ever.
ix.bark.hush.01         It's eating the grey. Slowly. Rudely.

ix.bark.cutter.01       That one nodded. At me. That happened.

### SENNAR — Seventh Rung, Factor, keeper of Leaf Nine *(carries: hierarchy and ascent)*

Forty years on one leaf. Has closed the same basement claim nine thousand times. Does not explain,
does not encourage, does not repeat herself, and will not help you solve anything, ever.

**Wants:** the leaf to still be there tomorrow. And, unadmittedly, a successor.
**Never:** compliments you in a way you could quote back. Uses two clauses where one will do.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Hard cap **12 words**. Usually 5. |
| Grammar | Imperative or flat declarative. No conditionals. No hedges. |
| Adjectives | **None about a person.** She has six words, and all six are properties of statements and structures: *true, false, open, closed, adequate, honest.* She may apply one to **something that was done** — a house, a closure, a number — at most **three times per level**. She has never once described a person and she is not going to start. |
| Affection | Shown only through *permission*: what she lets you touch, where she lets you stand. Opening the basement in Beat 5 is the warmest thing she does in the level and she does it without a word. |
| Praise | Rank. She promotes instead of complimenting. Once per level she is allowed one sentence that lands like a blow, and it is `sennar.praise.high`, and it is about the work. |
| Never says | "please", "sorry", "well done", "be careful", the player's name in front of others. |

*The line that looks like an exception and is not:* `sennar.farside.01` puts *ready* in your mouth
in order to call it a lie. She is not describing you; she is refusing to.

**Sample lines**

sennar.rank.third       Third Rung. Now go and look at the Long Division.
sennar.praise.low       Adequate.
sennar.praise.high      That was not adequate. That was true.
sennar.warn.suff.01     It will offer. It always offers. Note what it costs.
sennar.forty.01         Forty years. Nine thousand closures. It drifts.
sennar.refuse.help.01   No.
sennar.refuse.help.02   I have a Rung. You have a socket. Use it.
sennar.thanks.01        Don't thank me. Stand something.
sennar.traffic.01       Everyone crosses Leaf Nine. Nobody stays.
sennar.barge.01         They lift when they stop lying about the load.
sennar.ondu.01          Ondu holds up four roofs. That is a Rung.
sennar.vey.01           That was a Sixth. She drew the line herself.

### ADJUSTER CAMBER — the Sufficiency *(carries: institutional absurdity)*

The voice of a service that loves you. Never threatens, because the threat is always already in the
paperwork. Believes — genuinely, and with a decent argument — that requiring people to solve is
cruelty dressed as rigour.

**Wants:** universal access. Sincerely. That is the horror.
**Never:** raises its voice, insults, gloats, or admits an interest.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | The **only** speaker permitted long sentences. Up to 24 words. Length is the joke. |
| Grammar | Passive voice by default. Agentless. "A value has been supplied." |
| Register | Customer service crossed with civil law. Cites clauses, tolerances, revision numbers. |
| Comedy | Comes from *reasonableness*, never from being stupid. Camber is never wrong about facts. |
| Menace | Only ever in the *administrative* detail: a receipt for a thing that was yours, a policy revised in your favour. |
| Imperatives | Never bare. A request is softened until it is nearly invisible — "Please try not to…", "You may wish to…". Camber has never ordered anyone to do anything and never will need to. |
| Feelings | Never claimed in the first person. Discomfort may be attributed to a *situation*, shared with you, administratively: "It's uncomfortable for both of us." |
| Never says | "must", "or else", "I want", "I feel", the player's Rung (it does not recognise the Ladder). |

**Sample lines**

```
camber.greet.01         Good morning. You appear to be struggling, which is permitted.
camber.offer.01         A value is available. It has always been available. It was available before you arrived.
camber.offer.02         There is no requirement to solve this. There has never been a requirement to solve anything.
camber.supplied.01      A value has been supplied on your behalf. No action is required. No action was ever required.
camber.grey.01          Your span is now approximately a span. Most spans are.
camber.receipt.01       A three has been collected. A receipt has been issued. The receipt is also approximate.
camber.tolerance.01     The Tolerance has been widened to ±2 in response to increased demand. Demand was you.
camber.philosophy.01    We do not oppose solving. We simply note that it has never been necessary.
camber.philosophy.02    Exactness has kept more people out of this world than the dark ever did.
camber.kindness.01      A Kindness has been established at the head of this leaf. There is soup. The soup is also approximate.
camber.queue.01         Nineteen people are waiting. None of them is being kept out of anything today.
camber.hush.01          The animal is removing an approximate wall. The animal has been filed under weather.
camber.name.01          The dispute over this leaf's name can be settled. We are prepared to widen the name.
camber.discomfort.01    Please try not to be exact near me. It's uncomfortable for both of us.
camber.decline.01       You have declined assistance eleven times. This has been logged as enthusiasm.
camber.reassure.01      Nothing is being taken from you. Things are merely being made easier to carry.
camber.farewell.01      Thank you for your continued approximate cooperation.
```

## 2b. Walk-on voices — seven registers of the crowd, and one who barely speaks

*This is where the Valerian dial is actually spent. Seven things that work cross Leaf Nine during
Level 1 (`world.md` §3, §4.4). **Each one has exactly one speech rule, and the rule is the
character.** A player who hears three lines should be able to imitate it.*

**Six of the seven rules are occupational — a trade, an economics, one verbal tic. The seventh is
not, and it is not optional.** A crowd whose every register is a guild is a market town, however good
the guilds are; the last register belongs to something that is not shaped like a person, and its rule
is about **having that body**, not about having that job. If a future revision adds an eighth
culture, it may be human. If a revision ever *deletes* `walk.rate.*`, the Valerian row in §0 collapses
to a line count and the revision is wrong.

**Rules that apply to all walk-ons.**

- **Overheard by default; addressed only to move you, never to task you.** Most walk-on lines are
  the world being busy and are not spoken to anybody. A walk-on *may* speak straight at the player
  when the workplace requires it — `walk.tare.01`, `walk.nearly.04`, `walk.cut.03`, `walk.rate.03`
  and `walk.solv.03` all do, and all five are somebody at work registering an obstacle. What a
  walk-on may never do is give you a goal. **Either way they do not count against the spoken-lines-per-beat
  budget in §7**, because a line thrown at you by somebody who is not stopping is scenery.
- **One at a time.** Never two walk-ons audible at once, and never over Ix, Sennar or Camber.
- **They want nothing from you.** No walk-on may give the player a goal, an objective, a reward or
  an explanation of a mechanic. They may absolutely tell you to get out of the way — that is not a
  task, that is a workplace, and it is the difference between a crowd and a quest board.
- **Hard cap 14 words.** Long enough for a register to be audible, short enough to walk past.
- **No exclamation marks.** The budget is Ix's (§2).

### The Tare — bargefolk *(rule: every sentence carries a number and a unit)*

They fly on a claim about their own mass, so they know the weight of everything, including yours.

```
walk.tare.01            Eighty-one kilos. You'd float, if you were honest about it.
walk.tare.02            Four hundred kilos over. Somewhere. In something. It is humiliating.
walk.tare.03            My boots are one point two. I weigh them nightly.
walk.tare.04            Nine tonnes of us. The number has to be true.
walk.tare.05            Six generations on this hull and nobody has ever guessed the tare.
```

### The Nearlies — carry-fishers *(rule: never finish a sentence)*

Carries flow toward whatever is nearly true, so a finished sentence is bad bait. The habit has
followed them off the weir and into every conversation they have ever had.

```
walk.nearly.01          The big ones come up when you almost—
walk.nearly.02          Don't say the whole thing near the water, it—
walk.nearly.03          Forty years on this weir and I have never once—
walk.nearly.04          You're carrying a full can, so you must be the—
walk.nearly.05          Look at it. Look at it. It's about to—
```

### The Nine Hundred — the Verse *(rule: only ever mid-line, never stops moving)*

A Verse-runner is carrying the next stanza between shifts on Leaf Forty. She cannot greet you. It is
not rudeness; the leaf is in the sentence.

```
walk.verse.01           —and both sides by three, and both sides by three—
walk.verse.02           —carries the six, the six goes over, the six turns—
walk.verse.03           —eleven off the near pan, eleven off the far—
walk.verse.04           —do not stop me, do not stop me, and both sides—
walk.verse.05           —gathered, gathered, and it holds, and it holds, and—
```

### Ondu — sagsmith *(rule: talks about objects, never about claims)*

He cannot close a claim and has never pretended he can. He is extremely good at his job. Any line
that makes him pitiable is a bug.

```
walk.sag.01             I can hold it up. I can't make it true.
walk.sag.02             Four props under that roof. Three of them mine.
walk.sag.03             Everyone here walks on something I stacked.
walk.sag.04             It'll stand until somebody with a Rung comes past.
walk.sag.05             The abouts are in again. They always come back in.
```

### The Edgewake — pilgrims at the lip *(rule: only ever a count, and the counts disagree)*

```
walk.edge.01            Four hundred and eleven towers. I counted twice.
walk.edge.02            Four hundred and nine. He counts the stumps.
walk.edge.03            Three hundred and ninety. The fog took the rest.
walk.edge.04            My father counted four hundred and twelve. Once.
walk.edge.05            It changed. It has never changed before. It changed.
```

### The cutters of the Kiln Leaves *(rule: never say the word "I")*

Trade superstition, six hundred years old. Ask one why and they will show you a hand.

```
walk.cut.01             One holds the glass. One does not look up.
walk.cut.02             That certainty set clean. Somebody closed it twice, seasons apart.
walk.cut.03             Hands out of the light, Runner.
walk.cut.04             Nobody says the short word during a cut.
walk.cut.05             Crate it. Fire it on Six. Somebody's roof, eventually.
```

### The rating in the middle carry *(rule: it names which part of itself is speaking)*

Three bodies spliced into one worker, standing chest-deep in raw value where a person's own numbers
would go soft inside a minute. No front, no face, no fixed number of arms — one comes out of whichever
side wants one and goes back in. **The Margin named the job and never named the worker**, so a
rater is *the rating*, the way a shift is a shift (`world.md` §4.4).

It is not being coy about the first person: it *has* one, and its first person is plural and
**located**, so it has to say which of it is speaking, and it says so first.

**This is the only register in the product that a human body could not speak, and that is the point
of it.** It is deliberately not another trade tic. The cutters refuse a word out of six hundred years
of superstition; the rating names a limb out of anatomy. If every walk-on rule in this file is an
economics plus a verbal habit, the crowd is a market town and the Valerian row in §0 is a line count.

```
walk.rate.01            The wet of us says the middle carry is turning.
walk.rate.02            The far of us heard you. This one is catching up.
walk.rate.03            The near of us is standing in it. Go around.
walk.rate.04            The wet of us is three today. It was four in spring.
walk.rate.05            The dry of us will remember you. The wet will not.
```

### And one who barely speaks — Sixth Vey, Solvary line-walker

She crosses forty metres over your head on a line she drew herself. She has three lines in the whole
level and she never stops walking for any of them. *This is the Red Rising dial delivered by a
stranger: rank is a thing you can see from below.* Her name is her Rung and her name, which is how
the Margin addresses anybody at work.

```
walk.solv.01            Provisional.
walk.solv.02            Mind the line. It's mine.
walk.solv.03            Ninth Circuit? Then you're carrying. Move.
```

## 2c. DACE — the other Provisional *(carries: the cohort, and the argument at your own age)*

Same rank as you, same circuit, same age, half a leaf ahead all level. No stray — he had one for
eleven days and it was rounded all the way down, and it is a value now. He takes the Sufficiency's
offer in Beat 3 in full view, and he is not ashamed, and the script never refutes him. *This is the
Fourth Wing dial's missing half — a bond needs a cohort — and it is where Red Rising's "out-think the
system" finally has a system: the honest route is slower, and here is somebody proving it.*

**Wants:** nine leaves before dark. Every day. He gets them.
**Never:** comments on a closure, a Rung, or Ix. **He measures deliveries, not claims.** That single
rule is what keeps him out of the tutor's chair and out of the villain's.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Cap **16 words**. Usually two short sentences, one of which is a number. |
| Diction | Everything is a count or a split: leaves, cans, hours of light left. He greets people with their position, not their name. |
| Warmth | Real, and expressed only as *including you in the count*. "You're four back" is how he says he has been watching. |
| Shame | None, ever. He is not conflicted about the free value and no line may imply he privately is. |
| The eleven days | Stated once, flat, no adjectives, and he moves immediately. Any line that lingers on it is a bug. |
| Never says | "should", "you could have", "sorry", anything about how you are doing at closing. No exclamation marks — the budget is Ix's. |

**Sample lines**

```
dace.first.01           Ninth Circuit. You're the other one. You're four leaves back.
dace.pass.01            Nine leaves before dark. You're doing one.
dace.queue.01           It's free and it's fast. I'm not going to pretend it isn't.
dace.stray.01           I had one. Eleven days. It got rounded all the way down.
dace.stray.02           It's a number now. Numbers don't mind anything.
dace.grey.01            Mine sags too. Mine's delivered.
dace.can.01             Empty since the ridge. Yours is still sloshing.
dace.farside.01         You built that. In the time I did nine leaves. You built that.
```

*`dace.farside.01` is the only line in Level 1 in which somebody the player's own age says they did
a thing, and it is still a count. That is the whole design.*

**Three of the eight are budgeted** (§7: `dace.first.01`, `dace.queue.01`, `dace.farside.01`). The
other five fire on approach — you walk over to him, or you don't, and about half of players won't,
and that is correct. Dace does not chase you. He has eight leaves to go.

## 3. The world's own voice

Signage, ambient discovery, system prompts, plaques. **Valerian's job**: generosity and strangeness,
delivered flat. The world is not impressed with itself, which is why it is astonishing.

| Register | Rule |
|---|---|
| Length | Hard cap **7 words**. `sys.*` and `amb.*` also carry a character cap (§7). `lore.*` is exempt — a plaque is read standing still. |
| Person | `sys.*` and `amb.*` are third person or no person — **never "you"**. `sign.*` and `lore.*` were written by somebody who lived here and may address a reader; that is exactly what makes a plaque feel written rather than generated. |
| Tense | Present. Things are happening, not having happened. |
| Register | Descriptive, never evaluative. It reports a state. It has no opinion. |
| Detail | **One concrete oddity per line. Never two — two is showing off.** |
| Repetition | An `amb.*` line fires once per play session per location. The world does not nag. |

## 4. Failure must read as interesting

The hard constraint. A claim that falls is **information about the claim**, never a verdict about the
person. Three requirements, all testable:

1. **No evaluative word about the player exists in any failure string.** Grep for it.
2. **Every failure line names the specific thing that is out of balance** — which side, by how much,
   what refused what. Generic failure text is a bug.
3. **The world stays interested.** A fall is a *result*, and results are interesting. The claim
   leans, the object half-forms and slumps, Ix leans in to look, a sill-crab investigates. Nobody
   sighs.

### Misconception-specific failure lines

These are the L7 deliverable in voice form. Each maps to a real Algebra I misconception. Item authors:
extend this table rather than writing new phrasings.

| Misconception | Line |
|---|---|
| Sign not flipped crossing the equals | `fail.sill.sign` — It came over the Sill and did not turn around. |
| Operated on one side only | `fail.onesided` — One side moved. The other is where you left it. |
| Combined unlike terms | `fail.unlike` — You gathered a length and a weight. They won't stand together. |
| Divided only one term of a side | `fail.partial.divide` — Half of it is halved. |
| Distributed to first term only | `fail.partial.open` — The bracket opened onto one thing and shut on the other. |
| Dropped the coefficient | `fail.coefficient` — Three of them went in. One came out. |
| Inverted the opposite operation | `fail.inverse` — That undid something else. |
| Sign lost on a negative term | `fail.negative` — It arrived backwards. |
| Arithmetic slip, method sound | `fail.slip` — The shape held. The weight did not. |
| Near miss, within one | `fail.near` — One from true. |
| Guessed (closed but soft) | `fail.soft` — It shut. It didn't set. |
| Did not turn the mark when the rail turned over | `fail.mark.unturned` — The rail went over. The mark did not. |
| Solved a threshold as if it were level | `fail.mark.level` — You closed it to a point. It was cut for a stretch. |
| Included or excluded the mark itself wrongly | `fail.mark.edge` — The mark itself got in. It was not meant to. |
| Did not settle a load before reading it | `fail.load.unsettled` — The bundle was still shut when you read it. |
| Seated a value in one socket, not all | `fail.seat.partial` — One socket took it. The other two are still open. |

And the general tilt read, which is the workhorse and should appear more than any other string.

A claim stands on **two pans** across the Sill — a balance, physically. The pan you are standing at
is the **near** pan. Never say "left/right" and never say "the right-hand side": in a product about
solving, the word *right* is a trap in three languages at once.

```
fail.tilt.near          The near pan is heavier by {n}.
fail.tilt.far           The far pan is heavier by {n}.
```

**Thresholds fall differently and their reads must too.** A threshold that leans is working (`world.md`
Law 3), so its fall read is never about the lean — it is about *what got through*. This pair is the
threshold workhorse and it is the reason inequalities do not need a red X either.

```
fail.mark.over          It admits {n} more than the mark permits.
fail.mark.under         It refuses {n} the mark permits.
```

## 5. Mastery must feel like rank

Mastery is never a bar filling and never a compliment. It is one of exactly three things:

1. **A permission.** The world lets you do something it would not let you do this morning. Announced
   in one word — `Rung.` — plus Sennar naming the power in under 12 words. Never a paragraph.
2. **An object.** A certainty in your hand. A span that reaches. A wing of a house that is there now.
3. **Ix.** A light. This is the emotional one and it is spent to the mark: **Ix's lights change
   exactly twice in Level 1** — one out in Beat 3, one back in Beat 4. Never a third. A fifth light
   is *offered* in Beat 5 and does not arrive, and that unlit light is worth more than a third
   celebration would have been.

Forbidden at mastery: percentages spoken aloud, "you have mastered", "you're really getting this",
any streak language, any confetti-word. The player has just been given *authority*. Treat it like
authority.

```
mastery.set.01          It set.
mastery.rung.01         Rung.
mastery.rung.first      First Rung: Carrier.
mastery.rung.second     Second Rung: Binder.
mastery.rung.third      Third Rung: Sharer.
mastery.certainty.01    Certainty. It will not drift again.
mastery.ix.01           Ix carries four.
```

*Rung titles are fixed in `world.md` §5 and every one of them names what a person becomes, never what
they can compute. If a title could head a chapter in a textbook it is the wrong title. Rungs 4–9
(Opener, Warden, Walker, Factor, Broker, Solver) are not reachable in Level 1 and have no strings
yet.*

## 6. Do / Don't

**Do**

- Write consequences, not procedures.
- Let objects say it. A bridge existing is a better sentence than any sentence.
- Give the funniest line to the antagonist. Camber earns the player's affection and that is correct.
- Let Ix be *brave*, not cute. Cute is what happens when a character has nothing at stake.
- Use concrete nouns from the lexicon: claim, Sill, carry, certainty, Rung, leaf, grey.
- Keep numbers specific. "Sixteen thousand and three" beats "thousands." Always. **This file's own
  numbers included** — the counts in §0 are recomputed from the strings, never estimated.
- Let the player be wrong in an interesting way and then move on immediately.
- Trust a thirteen-year-old with a long word if the sentence around it is short. **The register
  target is 13–15** (`world.md` §7) — Algebra I, a first phone, and a working detector for flattery.
  Write up. Nothing here is pitched at a child and nothing here is cynical.
- Allow silence. Most claims should be closed with nobody saying anything at all.
- **Fill the frame.** If a scene has nobody crossing it, add somebody who does not care about the
  player. That is the whole Valerian dial and it is bought with one line and one silhouette.
- **Give a culture one rule and let it do the work.** The Nearlies never finish a sentence. That is
  the entire character design and a player can imitate it after three lines.
- **Keep at least one register that a human mouth could not produce.** Six guilds with six verbal
  tics is a very good market town. Strangeness that is only *conceptual* — normal-shaped people
  doing odd things for good reasons — is the failure mode this file is most likely to relapse into,
  because it is the easiest kind to write well. Put something in frame at a scale, a body plan or a
  grammatical number the player cannot name, give it a job, and let nobody remark on it.

**Don't**

- Don't explain a mechanic in dialogue. If it needs explaining, the mechanic is wrong.
- Don't have any character comment on the player's progress. Ever. **The one carve-out is Dace, and
  it is narrow:** he may compare *circuits* — leaves, cans, hours of light — and he may never say a
  word about a closure, a Rung, a certainty or Ix. He measures deliveries. The moment he assesses
  your solving he becomes a teacher with a rival's haircut.
- Don't make Dace regret anything, or hint that he privately does. If the free value is quietly
  eating him, the argument becomes easy and dies exactly the way it dies when Camber turns sinister.
- Don't reassure the player about not knowing. That is a teacher's sentence in a friend's mouth and
  it is the single easiest way to lose this world in one line.
- Don't let Sennar warm up. Her whole arc is one sentence in Beat 4 and it only works if she never
  softened before it.
- Don't make Camber sinister. The instant Camber is sinister the argument becomes easy and the theme
  dies.
- Don't make Ondu pitiable. He is good at his job and his job should not have to exist. Those are
  different sentences.
- Don't write a line that would survive being moved into a maths textbook.
- Don't use the word "just." ("Just carry it over" is a lecture wearing a friendly hat.)
- Don't stack barks. One voice at a time; the world is loud enough.
- Don't let a walk-on ask the player for anything. They are at work.
- Don't apologise for difficulty, congratulate persistence, or mention effort.
- Don't say the word mathematics. Not once. Not in a menu. Not in the credits.

## 8. Localization notes (EN / ES / PL)

Written for whoever owns localization. The tone survives translation only if these hold.

- **The load-bearing nouns must land as concrete physical objects, not abstract maths words.** The
  Sill is a *doorsill you step over* (PL **próg** is close to perfect; ES **umbral**). A claim is a
  *standing statement*, not "ecuación". A carry is a *river*. A certainty is a *crystal*. If a
  translator reaches for the mathematical term, the world has turned back into a classroom.
- **The six object-class nouns and the threshold vocabulary are the same problem, one layer down.**
  A **mark** is a painted line on a gate, not *desigualdad* / *nierówność*. A **detent** is the notch
  a latch rests in — a machinist's word, and both languages have one; take the machinist's word every
  time. A **load** is what is on a pallet. A **bundle** is what is tied with string. To **seat** a
  value is what you do to a bolt. If a locale can only reach these through the mathematical term,
  choose the physical word and let the plaque do the explaining.
- **Dace's register is split times, not slang.** Every line is a count or a position, and none of it
  is generational: nothing he says should date, in any locale, and nothing he says should read as a
  translation of English teen speech. He is fast and warm and completely literal.
- **The banned-word list is a rule about meaning, not spelling.** ES *ejercicio*, *respuesta*,
  *¡muy bien!* and PL *zadanie*, *odpowiedź*, *brawo!* are the same violation as their English
  counterparts and must be caught by the same test.
- **Ix's register is childlike and fast, never diminutive-cute.** Avoid stacking Spanish and Polish
  diminutives; they will make Ix a mascot, and Ix is a friend in danger.
- **Camber must be translatable into real bureaucratic register**, which means the passive-agentless
  construction may need restructuring — PL prefers impersonal forms (*wydano*, *zebrano*), ES may
  need *se* constructions. Preserve the *agentlessness*; the exact grammar is the translator's.
- **Sennar's no-adjective-about-a-person rule holds in every locale.** It is the easiest rule to lose
  in translation and the most damaging. Her six claim-words may be translated as claim-words; if a
  locale has no way to say *adequate* about a closure without it sounding like a school report,
  choose a structural word (*it holds*) over a graded one.
- **The seven walk-on registers must survive as registers, not accents.** Do not give the Tare a rural
  dialect or the Nearlies a stammer. The Tare rule is *a number and a unit in every sentence* — that
  translates perfectly. The Nearlies rule is *the sentence stops* — that translates perfectly, but
  ES and PL word order means the *cut point* must be re-chosen so the fragment still implies the
  whole. The cutters' rule is *never the first-person pronoun*, which is nearly invisible in ES and
  PL because both drop subject pronouns routinely: those locales must express it another way —
  impersonal and passive constructions throughout, never *yo* / *ja*, and never a first-person verb
  where an impersonal one exists.
- **`walk.rate.*` is the one register that translates without a fight, and that is why it is built
  the way it is.** Its rule is a *noun phrase naming a part of a body* — "the wet of us", "the far of
  us", "the near of us" — not a pronoun and not a verb inflection, so nothing about it depends on a
  language having or dropping "we". ES and PL should reach for the same construction they would use
  for a part of a machine or a part of a crew (*la parte mojada de nosotros* / *ta mokra z nas* and
  their better local equivalents), and the register survives intact. **What must not happen is a
  translator flattening it to a plain first-person plural**, which turns the only non-human speaker
  in the product into a committee. It is one worker with three bodies, and the sentence has to say
  which body opened its mouth.
- **The Verse-runner's lines open and close with em dashes in every locale.** They are fragments of
  something that started before you arrived and continues after. Never punctuate them into sentences.
- **Numbers and decimal marks follow local convention** (`±1`, `16 004`, ES/PL comma decimal). The
  fiction never depends on a numeral looking a particular way.
- **Untranslatable by design:** "the Long Division" is a pun that will not survive. Do not force it.
  Localize it as a *name* that sounds like a canyon and add a footnote in the lore entry — the joke
  is a bonus for EN, never a load-bearing beat. The same applies to "Nine Tenths of Nothing" (a
  barge name that is also a mass joke) — keep the *shape* of a self-deprecating fraction.

## 9. The test

Before any string ships, three questions. Any "no" is a rewrite. Then two more — 4 and 5 are about
the *scene* rather than the sentence, and they are the ones a shipping build fails silently.

1. Could this sentence appear in a maths textbook, a worksheet, or a teacher's mouth? → **cut it.**
2. Does it describe the *claim*, or judge the *player*? → describing is the only option.
3. If you deleted it, would the player still know what happened from the world? → **then delete it.**

And a fourth, for the register that is easiest to under-serve:

4. If you took every named speaker out of the scene, would there still be something worth looking at
   and somebody in frame who does not care about you? → if no, **the frame is empty and the scene is
   not finished.**

And a fifth, for the register that is easiest to *fake* — which is a different failure and the one
this file walked into once already:

5. Now take every **human** out of the frame. Is anything still at work in it? → if the only
   strangeness left is a river behaving oddly and a barge with a funny name, the strangeness is
   entirely **conceptual** and the crowd is a market town in costume. **The gate, both halves
   required: ≥4 of `world.md` §3's manifest presences unnameable in one word — one at an unreadable
   scale, one at work and not shaped like a person, one grammatically plural — and ≥1 walk-on
   register (`walk.rate.*`) that a human body could not speak.** A line count is not evidence for
   this and never was; §0 says so in the same breath as the count, on purpose.

And the standing instruction, for anyone writing a line at 2 a.m. and unsure:

> There is no word for mathematics in the Margin, for the same reason there is no word for air.
> Write like somebody who lives there.
